# 6. The editing pass

Even a well-prompted draft needs one human pass. Do these in order: each one is quick and catches a different problem.

1. **Run `prose-check`.** Clear the warnings, look at the info findings, ignore what does not apply. Mechanical problems out of the way first.
2. **Cut the first paragraph** and read from the second. Keep the cut only if the piece is worse without it.
3. **Read it aloud.** Split sentences you can't say in one breath. Rewrite phrases you would never say to a colleague.
4. **Replace adjectives with evidence.** "Incredibly fast" → "imports 10,000 rows in under three minutes" (measured, with the setup stated).
5. **Remove repeated claims.** List the three or four main points; delete any later restatement that adds nothing new.
6. **Add what only you know.** One real observation per major section: a mistake you made, a surprising result, a case where the advice does not apply. Only real ones.
7. **Check the ending.** Does it give the reader something to do or decide? If it summarises, cut back to the last useful sentence.
8. **Verify the claims list** ([Accuracy](05-accuracy.md)) and search for `[NEEDS:`.

## Targeted fixes, not rewrites

When a reviewer, editor or tool points at a problem, fix that problem. Rewriting the whole piece to address one comment loses the parts that were already right: the specific names, numbers and observations that took effort to gather.
